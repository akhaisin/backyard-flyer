# PID control fundamentals

A **PID controller** drives a system's output toward a desired target. It does so by computing the error between target and actual output, and emitting a control signal made of three terms:

- **P** — proportional to current error
- **I** — proportional to accumulated error over time
- **D** — proportional to the rate of change of error

## Behaviour to try

- Start with **Kp = 1, Ki = 0, Kd = 0**. The output rises toward the target but settles slowly with a small steady-state error.
- Crank **Kp** up to 5 or more. The response gets faster — but oscillates as Kp climbs.
- Add **Kd** to damp the oscillation. The output settles more cleanly.
- Add **Ki** to eliminate the steady-state offset. Watch out for windup at high values.
- Reduce **Mass** or **Damping** in the plant to make the system "twitchier" — gains that were stable may no longer be.
