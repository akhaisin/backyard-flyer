# Quad Pole — acro-mode coordinated turn around a flagpole

**Quad Pole** is a playground for FPV-style scripted maneuvers split along the real-world human/RC/FC boundary:

- **mission** ("human") — high-level: arm, fly to a waypoint, then run a named maneuver, then RTH and land.
- **planner_wp + navigator_wp / planner_cturn + navigator_cturn** ("RC pilot") — convert intent to AETR (Aileron-Elevator-Throttle-Rudder) sticks. Two stacks, one active per mission type.
- **fc_acro** ("flight controller") — single-mode rate loop. Thrust is a direct value; roll, pitch, and yaw are normalized body-rate commands.

```
mission ──► planner_wp     ──► navigator_wp     ─┐
        └─► planner_cturn ──► navigator_cturn ─┤
                                                  ├──► fc_acro ──► hw ──► world
                                              (one writes aetr;
                                               the other passes through)
```

The active stack is selected by `mission.missionType` (0 = WP, 1 = CTURN). Inactive blocks see the type, see it's not theirs, and pass `aetr` through unchanged.

## Mission steps and type

The STEPS array has two entries; `missionType` flips when the active step changes:

| Step | Kind | missionType | Notes |
|------|------|-------------|-------|
| 0 | waypoint | WP (0) | (8, 3, +3) — staging east of pole |
| 1 | maneuver | CTURN (1) | sweep-left, 5 s (100 ticks) |

## AETR sticks

The single bus between navigators and fc_acro. During the WP segments navigator_wp writes here via a cascade (position-PID → desired attitude → rate command → stick). During the CTURN segment navigator_cturn writes here from the sampled maneuver plan. `aetr.roll/pitch/yaw` are normalized desired body rates, so `aetr.yaw > 0` means yaw-right on the vehicle and in the stick overlay.

## coordinated-turn planner

Owns the maneuver library and the keyframe sampler. When `missionType !== CTURN` it emits zeros with `active = 0`. Edit the keyframe values here to change the maneuver shape.

## fc_acro

Single-mode, always rate-PID. No attitude memory. The `MAX_RATE_*` constants here define what "stick = 1" means in rad/s; upstream navigators use the same constants to normalize desired body rates onto the AETR bus.
